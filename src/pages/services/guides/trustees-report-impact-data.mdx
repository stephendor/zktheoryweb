# The trustees' report: the impact and volunteer data to collect now

<p class="lead">For treasurers, chief executives and finance or admin leads of charities in England and Wales with income of roughly £100,000 to £1.5 million.</p>

The rules for charity reports and accounts changed in 2026. The new Charities SORP asks the trustees' report to say more about impact and volunteers, and the thresholds for accounts and scrutiny have moved. The figures you'll need can't be pieced together reliably at year end. This guide sets out what to start recording now.

*This is a practical guide, not accounting or legal advice. Check the detail with your independent examiner or auditor.*

## What's changed and when

| Change | Applies from |
|---|---|
| <a href={SORP}>Charities SORP 2026</a> replaces SORP 2019 for charities that prepare accruals accounts | Reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026 |
| <a href={THRESHOLDS}>New thresholds</a>: accruals accounts above £500,000 income (was £250,000); independent examination above £40,000 (was £25,000); audit above £1.5 million (was £1 million), or above £500,000 with gross assets over £5 million | Financial years ending on or after 30 September 2026 |
| The Charity Commission's <a href={TAR_GUIDANCE}>guidance on preparing a trustees' annual report</a> updated for SORP 2026 | 16 September 2026 |

SORP 2026 sorts charities into three tiers by gross income. Each tier meets the requirements of the tiers below it as well as its own.

| SORP tier | Gross income |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Up to £500,000 |
| Tier 2 | £500,001 to £15 million |
| Tier 3 | Over £15 million |

Most charities this guide is for are in Tier 1 or Tier 2. If your financial year started on 1 April 2026, you're already in your first SORP 2026 year: its report, accounts and annual return will be due by 31 January 2028.

The deadline itself hasn't changed. The <a href={ANNUAL_RETURN}>annual return is due within 10 months</a> of your financial year end, so 31 January 2027 for a year ending 31 March 2026. If your income is over £25,000, you send the trustees' report and accounts with it; a CIO always does.

## What the report asks for

Two parts of the trustees' report depend on records you keep during the year (<a href={SORP}>SORP 2026, module 1</a>):

- **Impact.** The report explains what difference your work made to the people you work with, and whether there were wider benefits to society. That needs evidence of outcomes, not just activity.
- **Volunteers.** The report must explain the scale and nature of volunteer activities and what volunteers put in. You may also give the hours they contributed, or the staff equivalent, where that's practical.

## Why the data has to be collected during the year

At year end, someone opens last year's report, finds the figures that were used, and tries to repeat them from whatever records survive. Three things go wrong.

- **Volunteer hours can't be rebuilt.** If hours weren't logged as they happened, the year-end figure is a guess. A guess can't be checked, and you can't compare it with next year.
- **People and attendances get mixed up.** "Families supported" can mean referrals, families who came once, or families who stayed. Without one written definition, each report counts differently.
- **Outcomes need a starting point.** You can only show change if you recorded where people started. That has to happen when they join, not in March.

There's a fourth risk: your funders. Most end-of-grant reports ask for the same figures as the trustees' report, often at a different time of year. If the two don't agree, someone will ask why.

## What to record now: a checklist

- **Outputs against outcomes.** Record what you delivered (sessions, places, hours) separately from what changed for people (outcomes), and link each outcome to the activity behind it.
- **Who you reach: counts, not names.** Keep counts of people by the groups that matter to your aims, such as age band, area or referral route. Names stay in your case records, not in the reporting file.
- **Volunteer numbers, roles and hours.** Log volunteers by role, with hours recorded each week or month rather than estimated at year end.
- **One written definition for each figure.** Write down what counts as a participant, a session, an attendance and an outcome, and use the same definition everywhere.
- **Where each figure comes from.** For every number in the report, note the spreadsheet or system it comes from, who keeps it and the cut-off date.
- **The funder figures that need to agree.** List the figures you report to funders that also appear in the trustees' report, and check they match before either goes out.
